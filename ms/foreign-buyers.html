Buying Property in Malaysia as a Foreigner

Yes, foreigners can buy property in Malaysia, freehold included.

Each state sets a minimum price, the purchase needs state consent and stamp duty is charged at a flat rate. Check your state below, see which projects qualify, then ask Keith to confirm the unit you like.

Freehold or leasehold

Foreigners can own both, with a strata or individual title in their own name.

Minimum price by state

From RM500,000 to RM2 million or more, depending on the state and property type.

8% stamp duty

A flat rate on the transfer for foreign buyers from 1 January 2026.

State consent

Your lawyer applies for it after you sign. It adds a few weeks to a few months.

Costs

What it costs to buy and sell

Stamp duty on the transfer

8% of the price for foreign buyers (permanent residents excluded), on instruments of transfer from 1 January 2026.

Stamp duty on the loan

0.5% of the loan amount, if you take a Malaysian housing loan.

Legal fees and state consent

Lawyer fees follow a set scale. The state consent fee and processing differ by state; Keith gets you the exact figures.

Selling later (RPGT)

Real property gains tax for non-citizens: 30% if you sell within 5 years, 10% from the 6th year.

A general guide, not tax or legal advice. Rates are set by the government and can change; your lawyer confirms them for your purchase.

  3. 3

    Sign the sale and purchase agreement and pay the down payment. Signing can be arranged while you are overseas.

  4. 4
    State consent

    Your lawyer applies to the state authority for consent to the foreign purchase.

  5. 5

    Pay in cash or apply for a loan. Some Malaysian banks lend to foreigners, usually with a lower financing margin than for locals.

    For a new launch, payments follow the construction stages until vacant possession and the keys.

FAQ

Questions foreign buyers ask Keith

Can foreigners buy property in Malaysia?

Yes. Foreigners can buy freehold and leasehold homes if the price meets the state minimum and the state gives consent. Bumiputera lots, low-cost and affordable housing are not open to foreigners.

Is there a minimum price?

Yes, each state sets one, for example RM1 million in Kuala Lumpur and RM2 million in Selangor. Use the checker above, and ask Keith to confirm the exact figure for the unit you like.

Can I get a housing loan as a foreigner?

Some Malaysian banks do lend to foreigners. The financing margin is usually lower than for Malaysians and depends on your income, country and the bank. Keith can introduce you to bankers who handle foreign buyers.

Does buying a home give me a visa?

No. A property purchase on its own does not come with residency. If you want to live in Malaysia, look at MM2H, which is a separate application.

Can I buy without coming to Malaysia?

Much of it can be done remotely: video viewings, booking online and signing documents overseas with your lawyer. Many buyers still visit once before or after booking.

Can I rent the unit out?

Yes. You can rent your home out on a normal tenancy. Rental income earned in Malaysia is taxable; non-residents are taxed at a flat rate.

What taxes do I pay when I sell?

Real property gains tax for non-citizens is 30% on the gain if you sell within 5 years and 10% from the 6th year.

Which states are not listed?

Terengganu, Kelantan, Sarawak and other states set their own rules. WhatsApp Keith and he will check the current position for the project you are looking at.

Not sure if a unit qualifies?

Send Keith the project and unit, or just your budget and state. He will confirm the minimum, the costs and which units you can buy.
