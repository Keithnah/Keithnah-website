MM2H vs Thailand LTR Visa: Assets, Income and Who Each One Suits

Thailand's Long-Term Resident visa is built around wealth or income tests rather than a deposit and a home. How it compares with MM2H.

By Keith Nah · 5 min read
Thailand LTR10 years
LTR feeAbout THB 50,000
Wealthy trackUSD 1 million in assets
MM2H SilverUSD 150,000 deposit
01

Different tests

Thailand's LTR visa offers a 10-year stay to people who pass wealth, income or professional tests. The Wealthy Global Citizen track is commonly described as needing about USD 1 million in assets, with part invested in Thailand. Other tracks are based on pension or work income.

02

What MM2H asks instead

MM2H does not test your income in the same way. It asks for a fixed deposit and a home purchase in Malaysia. For people with savings but modest ongoing income, MM2H can be easier to qualify for.

03

Costs

The LTR's government fee is reported at around THB 50,000 per person, low compared with Thailand's Privilege membership. But the asset or income bar is high. MM2H's money mostly stays yours as a deposit and a property.

04

Which fits you

High earners and well-paid remote workers may find the LTR attractive. Families who want to own a home and settle long term may prefer MM2H. Both have changed their rules recently, so check the current criteria.

Keith's take

When clients compare the two, I ask what they want to own. The LTR is a visa; MM2H is a visa plus a home in your name. For families planning to stay ten years or more, owning the home usually wins.
