KL subsale average tops RM1.2m as luxury deals lift prices

The average price of a subsale home in Kuala Lumpur rose 52% from a year earlier to about RM1.2 million in the second quarter of 2026, according to Juwai IQI. The jump came mainly from deals of RM4 million and above, which rose by about half. In the first quarter, the city's average was about RM1.02 million. The typical KL buyer paid much less than that. Juwai IQI says the median subsale price in Kuala Lumpur rose 12.8% to RM530,000, and the national median rose 2.7% to RM380,000, its third quarterly rise in a row. Nearly seven in ten subsale purchases nationwide were priced at RM500,000 or below. The picture differs by state: Penang's median rose 17.1%, partly because a different mix of homes changed hands, while Selangor edged slightly lower. Juwai IQI expects more transactions in the second half of the year, with prices rising moderately and Johor doing best. Keep in mind this is an agency's sales data, not the official national index.

Keith's take

Do not let the RM1.2 million headline scare you, or excite you. An average jumps when a few very expensive homes sell, which is why the median, about RM530,000 in KL, is the number that describes what most people actually paid. For home buyers, this means good homes in Kuala Lumpur are still within reach for many budgets, but you need to compare prices building by building, not city by city. For investors, rising luxury deals show money is flowing into prime areas, but rent in those segments does not always rise as fast as prices, so check the yield before you buy. My checklist: look up recent transacted prices for the exact building and unit size, compare new launches nearby against subsale prices, ask what the realistic rent is today, and set your ceiling price before you view. Buying Guide

Buying property in Malaysia, step by step.

Pick the guide that matches your purchase. Each one covers the key numbers, the six stages and the questions buyers ask most.

Buying a new property in Malaysia

Short answer

A new launch follows a fixed legal route. You pay a booking fee, sign the developer's statutory Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA) with 10% paid, then your bank pays the developer in stages as the building goes up. The developer must hand over within 36 months for a condo (24 months for landed), or pay you late-delivery damages, and must fix defects for 24 months after handover.

Key numbers at a glance

Sale and Purchase AgreementStatutory form under the Housing Development Act: Schedule H for strata (condos), Schedule G for landed. The developer cannot change its terms against you.
Paid when you sign the SPA10% of the price, including the booking fee you already paid
Booking feeUsually 2% to 3%, credited towards the 10%
Handover deadline36 months for strata, 24 months for landed, counted from the SPA date
Late delivery damages (LAD)10% a year of the purchase price, counted from the date you paid the booking fee
Defect liability period24 months from vacant possession; the developer must repair within 30 days of your written notice
Before you get the keysA Certificate of Completion and Compliance (CCC), plus connected water and electricity
If something goes wrongThe Tribunal for Homebuyer Claims hears claims of up to RM50,000 for a small filing fee

Six stages, start to finish

  1. 1Shortlist and check
  2. 2Book and arrange the loan
  3. 3Sign the SPA and loan agreement
  4. 4Pay in stages during construction
  5. 5Vacant possession and handover
  6. 6After you get the keys
Stage 1

Shortlist and check

  • Check the developer's licence and advertising permit numbers, which must appear on every advertisement, on the housing ministry's website.
  • Confirm the land title: residential, or commercial. Serviced apartments on commercial land usually pay commercial rates for utilities and assessment tax.
  • Compare the price per sq ft and maintenance fee with completed projects nearby, and look up the developer's past projects and delivery record.
  • Ask for the full price list, the rebates, and what is included: car parks, built-in fittings and legal fee promotions.
Stage 2

Book and arrange the loan

  • Sign the booking form and pay the booking fee. Read the refund terms first: some developers keep part of the fee if you cancel.
  • Apply to two or three banks at the same time. Banks look at your debt service ratio (monthly debts against income).
  • Bank Negara caps the loan at 70% for your third and later home loans; first and second homes can usually go up to 90%.
  • Prepare your IC or passport, latest salary slips or tax returns, and three to six months of bank statements.
Stage 3

Sign the SPA and loan agreement

  • Sign the SPA within the time stated on your booking form, and pay the balance up to 10% of the price.
  • Check the unit number, size, car park bays, payment schedule and delivery date in the SPA before you sign.
  • Sign the loan agreement with the bank's lawyer. Loan stamp duty is 0.5% of the loan amount.
  • Developers often pay the SPA legal fees in their promotions; the loan legal fees are usually yours.
Stage 4

Pay in stages during construction

  • The developer bills by construction stage, as set out in the SPA payment schedule.
  • Your bank releases each payment directly to the developer once the architect certifies the stage.
  • You pay interest only on the amount released so far, so instalments start small and rise as the building goes up.
  • Keep every progress bill and payment receipt: you will need them for the final stages and any claim.
Stage 5

Vacant possession and handover

  • The developer sends a notice of vacant possession once the CCC is issued and water and electricity are connected.
  • Pay the final amounts due, then collect your keys and the handover documents.
  • Inspect the unit carefully on the day, photograph every defect and submit a written defects list.
  • Maintenance fees, sinking fund, utilities and (once issued) assessment tax start from vacant possession.
Stage 6

After you get the keys

  • For 24 months the developer must repair defects you report in writing, within 30 days of your notice.
  • If they do not, you may repair it yourself after giving notice and claim the cost, or go to the Tribunal for Homebuyer Claims.
  • For condos, the Joint Management Body runs the building until the strata titles are issued and the Management Corporation takes over.
  • Claim late delivery damages if the handover came after the deadline in your SPA.

Questions buyers ask

Do I get my booking fee back if I cancel?

It depends on the booking form. Before the SPA is signed, many developers refund the fee in full or keep a small administration charge. Read the cancellation terms before you pay, and keep the receipt.

How much can I claim for late delivery?

Late delivery damages are 10% a year of the purchase price, counted from the date you paid the booking fee until vacant possession. For a RM800,000 condo delivered three months late, that is about RM20,000.

How long is the defect liability period?

24 months from vacant possession. Report defects in writing; the developer must repair them within 30 days of your notice.

Where can I complain about a developer?

Start with a written complaint to the developer. If that fails, file a claim with the Tribunal for Homebuyer Claims (up to RM50,000) or complain to the housing ministry. Larger claims go to court.

Should I buy a new launch or a subsale unit?

A new launch usually costs less upfront and you pay in stages, but you wait two to four years and rely on the developer. A subsale unit is ready now and you can inspect it, but you pay more upfront and take it as it is. Many investors like new launches for capital growth, and subsale units for immediate rental income.

Buying a subsale property in Malaysia

Short answer

A subsale is a completed home bought from its current owner. Most of the risk is settled before you pay anything: check the title, the owner's arrears and the condition of the unit first. You then pay an earnest deposit (usually 2% to 3%) to hold the unit, sign the SPA with 10% paid, and the lawyers complete the sale in about three months.

Key numbers at a glance

Earnest depositUsually 2% to 3% with your offer letter, topped up to 10% when you sign the SPA
Completion periodUsually 3 months from the SPA (or from the date consent is given), plus a 1-month extension with late-payment interest, commonly 8% a year
Stamp duty on transferMalaysians and PRs: 1% on the first RM100,000, 2% up to RM500,000, 3% up to RM1,000,000 and 4% above, on the higher of the price or market value. Foreigners: flat 8% on residential from 1 Jan 2026
Stamp duty on the loan0.5% of the loan amount
RPGT retentionYour lawyer holds back 3% of the price (7% if the seller is not a Malaysian citizen) and pays it to LHDN within 60 days
State consentNeeded for leasehold titles or titles with a restriction in interest, and for foreign buyers; it can add weeks or months
Agent commissionUp to 3% of the price, normally paid by the seller
Check before you offerTitle search, caveats and charges, maintenance and assessment arrears, tenancy and the unit's condition

Six stages, start to finish

  1. 1Check before you make an offer
  2. 2Make an offer and pay the deposit
  3. 3Sign the SPA
  4. 4Consent and loan documents
  5. 5Completion
  6. 6Getting the keys
Stage 1

Check before you make an offer

  • Ask a lawyer for a land title search: owner's name, title type, any bank charge, caveats and restrictions such as Bumiputera or Malay Reserve status.
  • Ask whether an individual strata title has been issued. Without it, the transfer needs the developer's consent and takes longer.
  • Check arrears: maintenance and sinking fund, quit rent, assessment tax and utility bills. Agree in writing that the seller clears them.
  • Inspect the unit in daylight: leaks, cracks, water pressure, wiring and any renovation that may need approval.
Stage 2

Make an offer and pay the deposit

  • Write the offer letter with the price, what furniture is included, the completion period and "subject to loan approval".
  • Pay the earnest deposit, usually 2% to 3%, to the agent or lawyer as stakeholder, not directly to the seller.
  • Apply for your loan now. The bank will send a valuer, and the loan is based on the lower of price or valuation.
  • If the valuation comes in low, you pay the difference in cash or renegotiate.
Stage 3

Sign the SPA

  • Sign the SPA, usually within two to three weeks of the offer, and pay the balance up to 10% of the price.
  • Your lawyer stamps the SPA and files the RPGT forms with LHDN.
  • Your lawyer holds back 3% of the price (7% if the seller is not a citizen) for the seller's RPGT.
  • Legal fees follow the Solicitors' Remuneration Order scale. Use our calculator to estimate them.
Stage 4

Consent and loan documents

  • Where needed, your lawyer applies for state consent or the developer's or management's consent to transfer.
  • Sign the loan agreement. Your bank gives an undertaking to pay the balance on completion.
  • Pay the stamp duty on the Memorandum of Transfer and on the loan.
  • If the seller still has a loan, your bank pays it off directly and the rest goes to the seller.
Stage 5

Completion

  • The balance of the price must be paid within the completion period, usually 3 months plus a 1-month extension.
  • If the bank pays late, interest is usually charged at around 8% a year for the extension period.
  • Your lawyer registers the transfer (Form 14A) and the bank's charge (Form 16A) at the land office. Registration can take a few months.
Stage 6

Getting the keys

  • The seller hands over vacant possession and the keys once the full price is paid.
  • Take meter readings and transfer water, electricity and internet accounts to your name.
  • Register with the management office and update the names for quit rent and assessment tax.
  • Keep your stamped documents and the final title safe: you need them when you sell.

Questions buyers ask

How much deposit do I pay for a subsale property?

Usually 2% to 3% as an earnest deposit with your offer, then the balance up to 10% when you sign the SPA. The other 90% is paid on completion, usually through your bank loan.

How long does a subsale take?

About three months from the SPA to completion, plus a one-month extension if needed. Titles that need state or developer consent can take longer.

Why does my lawyer hold back 3% for LHDN?

The law requires the buyer to retain part of the price so the seller's Real Property Gains Tax is covered: 3% for Malaysian sellers and 7% for non-citizen sellers. It is paid to LHDN within 60 days and counts towards the seller's tax.

What should I check before buying a subsale property?

The title (owner, charges, caveats and restrictions), whether a strata title has been issued, the owner's arrears, any tenancy, and the condition of the unit, including approvals for renovations.

Who pays the agent's commission?

Normally the seller. The legal maximum is 3% of the price. If you appoint your own agent to find a property, agree the fee in writing first.

Foreigners buying property in Malaysia

Short answer

Foreigners can own freehold and leasehold homes in Malaysia, but only above a minimum price set by each state, and every purchase needs state consent. From 1 January 2026, residential transfers to non-citizens pay a flat 8% stamp duty. Banks usually lend 50% to 70% of the price.

Key numbers at a glance

Who counts as a foreign buyerAnyone who is not a Malaysian citizen. Permanent residents pay local stamp duty rates; check whether your state's threshold and consent rules apply to you
Minimum purchase priceSet by each state: Kuala Lumpur RM1,000,000; Selangor RM2,000,000; Johor RM1,000,000 for strata and RM2,000,000 for landed; Penang varies by island, mainland and property type
What you cannot buyMalay Reserve land, Bumiputera lots, low- and medium-cost housing, and units below the state's minimum price
State consentRequired for every foreign purchase; a transfer without it is void. Lawyers apply after the SPA, and fees and time vary by state
Stamp duty on transferFlat 8% on residential property from 1 Jan 2026, plus 0.5% on the loan
Home loanTypically 50% to 70% of the price, depending on the bank and the country your income comes from
Rental income taxNon-residents pay a flat 30% on Malaysian rental income and file a yearly return
RPGT when you sell30% of the gain within 5 years, 10% from the 6th year; the buyer holds back 7% of the price

Six stages, start to finish

  1. 1Confirm you are eligible
  2. 2Budget and financing
  3. 3Book and sign the SPA
  4. 4Get state consent
  5. 5Completion and transfer
  6. 6Owning, renting and selling
Stage 1

Confirm you are eligible

  • Check the minimum price for the state and property type you want. Prices quoted before rebates may not count.
  • Make sure the unit is not on Malay Reserve land, a Bumiputera lot or low- or medium-cost housing.
  • Ask whether the developer or the title needs extra approval for foreign buyers.
Stage 2

Budget and financing

  • Budget around 10% to 12% on top of the price: 8% stamp duty, legal fees, consent fees and loan costs.
  • Ask banks that lend to your nationality for a pre-approval: usually 50% to 70% of the price.
  • Prepare your passport, proof of income (salary slips, employment letter or tax returns), bank statements and a credit report from your home country.
  • Keep records of the money you send into Malaysia: you will need them to send sale proceeds home later.
Stage 3

Book and sign the SPA

  • Pay the booking fee (new launch) or earnest deposit (subsale).
  • Sign the SPA, usually stated as subject to state consent, and pay the balance up to 10%.
  • You can sign through a lawyer or with a power of attorney if you are overseas.
Stage 4

Get state consent

  • Your lawyer applies to the state authority once the SPA is signed.
  • It can take from a few weeks to several months, depending on the state.
  • The completion period in the SPA usually starts only once consent is granted.
Stage 5

Completion and transfer

  • Pay the balance of the price, through your bank loan or funds sent from overseas.
  • Pay the 8% stamp duty on the transfer. Your lawyer registers the property in your name.
  • Collect the keys and set up utilities and the management account.
Stage 6

Owning, renting and selling

  • Pay maintenance fees, sinking fund, quit rent and assessment tax every year.
  • If you rent it out, check the building's rules on short stays, and declare the rental income: non-residents pay 30% on it.
  • When you sell, RPGT is 30% of the gain within 5 years and 10% from the 6th year. Holding longer saves tax.

Questions buyers ask

What is the minimum price for foreigners?

It depends on the state: RM1,000,000 in Kuala Lumpur, RM2,000,000 in Selangor, and in Johor RM1,000,000 for strata and RM2,000,000 for landed. Penang sets different limits for the island, the mainland and each property type. Always confirm the current rule before you book.

How much stamp duty do foreigners pay?

A flat 8% of the price on residential property transferred from 1 January 2026 (Malaysians pay 1% to 4% in tiers). There is also 0.5% stamp duty on a loan.

Do permanent residents count as foreigners?

For stamp duty, Malaysian PRs pay local rates. For minimum prices and consent, rules differ by state, so check with your lawyer before you book.

Can foreigners get a home loan in Malaysia?

Yes. Several banks lend to foreigners, usually 50% to 70% of the price, depending on your nationality, income source and credit history.

How much RPGT do foreigners pay when selling?

30% of the gain if you sell within 5 years, and 10% from the 6th year. The buyer's lawyer holds back 7% of the price for LHDN.
