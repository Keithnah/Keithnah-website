Latest Airbnb Ban in Penang Residential Developments

30 Sep 2026 · Keith Nah

Penang has tightened its stance on short-term rentals in residential developments in 2026, moving to restrict Airbnb-style listings within many purely residential strata schemes. For property owners and prospective buyers eyeing entry into short-term rental as an investment strategy, this shift matters more than a single line of news — it reshapes the underlying value proposition of some units entirely.

What the Restriction Covers

The restriction targets residential-only zoned strata title developments, differentiating them from hotel-zoned or serviced-residence-zoned developments — SoHo, SoVo, or serviced apartments — which continue to allow short-term letting under their commercial or hotel licence. Pure-residential title condos are where local councils and management corporations have moved to enforce existing residential-use rules more strictly.

Enforcement generally follows a complaints-driven process at the management corporation or JMB level, working alongside local council bylaws. That means existing operators in strictly residential buildings typically face growing pressure through MC warnings, fines or utility restrictions, rather than a single blanket state-wide law banning short-term rental outright everywhere.

Why This Matters for Investors

Units bought specifically for short-term rental yield in purely residential schemes may now need to pivot toward long-term leasing, which resets the yield assumptions many buyers used to justify premium purchase prices during the earlier short-term rental boom.

Developments explicitly zoned and licensed for short-term or serviced use become comparatively more valuable for that specific investment strategy, since they sit outside the new restriction and continue operating under their existing commercial provisions.

What Buyers Should Check Before Committing

Confirm land title and zoning classification — residential versus commercial or hotel — before assuming a unit can legally run short-term rentals, and check the specific management corporation's by-laws rather than relying on general area reputation.

For existing owners in affected buildings, converting to long-term rental or owner-occupation are the realistic paths forward. It's worth stress-testing whether the original purchase price still makes sense under a long-term-lease yield rather than the short-term assumption it may have been bought under.
