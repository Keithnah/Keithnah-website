RTS Link Explained: What Singapore-JB Commuters Need to Know

16 Jun 2026 · Keith Nah

The RTS Link is the rail connection designed to link Bukit Chagar in Johor Bahru with Woodlands North in Singapore, aimed squarely at cutting the daily crossing time for the tens of thousands of people who commute between the two cities. Here's what commuters — and property buyers thinking about commuters as future tenants — actually need to know.

The Route And What It Connects

The RTS Link connects a station in central JB directly to Singapore's Woodlands North MRT station, which itself sits on Singapore's Thomson-East Coast Line — meaning a JB-based commuter can, in principle, reach much of central Singapore via a single cross-border rail journey plus a domestic MRT connection, without touching the causeway's road traffic at all.

On the JB side, the interchange has been designed to integrate with local transport options, which matters for property buyers assessing whether a specific project is genuinely "RTS-accessible" or simply within the same general district as the station — the difference between a five-minute walk and a twenty-minute drive to the interchange is significant for both livability and rental appeal.

What It Means For Daily Commuters

For Singapore-based workers who live in JB, or JB-based workers commuting into Singapore, the RTS Link's core promise is predictability — replacing a causeway crossing that can vary wildly by time of day with a scheduled rail service. That predictability is what makes a JB base genuinely viable for someone with a fixed start time at a Singapore workplace, in a way that causeway-dependent commuting rarely allows.

It's worth noting that a rail crossing doesn't eliminate the need to consider onward travel time within each city — a station-adjacent commute in JB still needs to be paired with reasonable onward transport in Singapore, which is where the Thomson-East Coast Line connection becomes relevant to how far into Singapore a JB-based commute genuinely reaches.

Why This Matters To Property Buyers, Not Just Commuters

For buyers considering a JB property as a rental investment, understanding the actual commuter experience — not just the marketing description — is essential, since the tenant pool most likely to pay a premium for an RTS-adjacent unit is precisely the cross-border commuter segment this line is built to serve.

We generally advise clients to think about RTS-adjacent property the way they would think about any transit-oriented investment: the closer and more genuinely walkable the access, the more durable the rental premium tends to be over time, versus units that are merely in the same general area as the connectivity story.
