Comparing Property Prices: Singapore vs Kuala Lumpur vs Johor Bahru

12 Jun 2026 · Keith Nah

For buyers weighing where their money goes furthest, comparing Singapore, Kuala Lumpur and Johor Bahru side by side makes the regional price gap concrete. The differences aren't just about raw price per square foot — financing, ownership rules and ongoing costs all shift the real comparison meaningfully.

The Price-Per-Square-Foot Gap

Even in Singapore's more accessible suburban districts, price per square foot for a comparable condominium typically runs at a significant multiple of what the same finish quality and building standard commands in KL City Centre or Bangsar — and the gap widens further when comparing to JB, where new-launch pricing remains a fraction of both Singapore and KL prime-district levels.

This gap isn't purely about location desirability — it also reflects fundamentally different land scarcity dynamics. Singapore is a city-state with genuinely finite land; KL and JB both have considerably more room to expand outward, which keeps a lid on land-cost-driven price escalation in a way Singapore structurally cannot replicate.

Ownership Rules Change The Real Comparison

Singapore's Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty regime adds a substantial surcharge for foreign buyers on top of the base purchase price, meaningfully changing the effective cost of entry compared to the headline listing price. Malaysia has no ABSD, but foreign buyers pay a flat 8% stamp duty on residential property from 1 January 2026 (Malaysians pay 1% to 4%), and state consent fees and minimum purchase thresholds still apply and vary by state.

This means a straight price-per-square-foot comparison between the three markets understates the real cost gap for a foreign buyer specifically — once Singapore's additional stamp duty is factored in, the effective premium for buying there versus KL or JB is considerably larger than the headline price difference alone suggests.

What This Means For A Cross-Border Portfolio

For investors building a Southeast Asian property portfolio rather than a single home purchase, this price gap is exactly why KL and JB have become increasingly common complements to a Singapore-anchored portfolio — the lower entry cost allows for either a larger footprint per dollar deployed, or capital freed up to diversify across more properties rather than concentrating into a single high-cost Singapore asset.

The trade-off, as with any cross-border allocation, is liquidity and market depth — Singapore's resale market remains deeper and more liquid than either KL or JB's, which is a genuine consideration for investors who may need to exit a position on a shorter timeline than a full market cycle.
