Foreign Ownership Rules in Malaysia: A State-by-State Breakdown

8 Jun 2026 · Keith Nah

Foreign ownership rules in Malaysia aren't uniform across the country — each state sets its own minimum purchase price threshold and, in some cases, additional restrictions on property type. Understanding your target state's specific rules before you start browsing listings will save a significant amount of wasted time.

Why Thresholds Differ By State

Malaysia's foreign ownership framework operates at both federal and state level — the federal government sets broad principles, but each state government has authority to set its own minimum purchase price for foreign buyers, and these thresholds can differ substantially between states depending on local market conditions and each state's own housing policy priorities.

Kuala Lumpur, as a federal territory rather than a state, sets its own threshold directly, which currently sits at RM1 million for most property types — a figure that has remained relatively stable in recent years compared to some states that have adjusted their thresholds more frequently in response to local market conditions.

How Selangor, Penang And Johor Compare

Selangor's threshold varies by specific zone within the state, generally sitting in a broadly similar range to KL's for prime areas but with some flexibility for other zones. Penang, given its island geography and land scarcity, has historically maintained one of the higher thresholds among the states, reflecting genuine land constraints on Penang Island specifically versus the more spacious mainland side.

Johor has structured its rules with particular attention to the JB-Singapore corridor, given the scale of cross-border interest from Singaporean buyers — buyers should confirm the current specific threshold for their target district, since Johor has adjusted its framework more than once in response to demand patterns along the RTS Link corridor.

Practical Advice For Cross-State Buyers

If you're comparing properties across multiple states — say, KL and JB side by side — it's worth confirming both thresholds and any additional state consent requirements before shortlisting units, since a property that looks attractively priced against KL's RM1 million benchmark may sit above or below a different state's threshold entirely.

State consent processing times can also differ meaningfully, with some states processing foreign purchase consent considerably faster than others. This is exactly the kind of detail that a locally experienced agent should be able to walk you through before you commit to a specific unit, rather than discovering it mid-transaction.
