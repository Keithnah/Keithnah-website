TRX: Inside Malaysia's New Financial District and Its Property Market

22 May 2026 · Keith Nah

TRX — Tun Razak Exchange — represents Malaysia's most ambitious purpose-built financial district to date, and its property market is still in the relatively early stages of establishing what it will ultimately become as the district fills out over the coming years.

A District Built From The Ground Up

Unlike districts that evolved organically over decades, TRX was master-planned from the outset as an integrated financial and lifestyle district — purpose-built office towers for major financial institutions sit alongside residential towers, retail, and green public space, all designed together rather than accumulated piecemeal over time.

This master-planned approach gives TRX a level of infrastructure cohesion that's genuinely difficult for older, organically-grown districts to match — the MRT interchange, road networks and public spaces were all designed in tandem with the buildings, rather than retrofitted around them after the fact.

The Corporate Tenant Base Reshaping The Market

As major financial institutions and multinational corporations have progressively moved staff into TRX's office towers, the district has begun building exactly the kind of corporate tenant base that supports strong residential rental demand — professionals who want to live within walking distance of their workplace, and are often supported by corporate housing allowances that make premium rents more accessible.

This corporate-driven demand base is qualitatively different from tourism-linked or purely lifestyle-driven demand elsewhere in KL, and it's part of why TRX residential rents have held up well even while the surrounding retail and public amenity mix is still maturing.

What Early Investors Are Betting On

Investors buying into TRX today are essentially betting on the district completing its build-out roughly as planned — more office towers reaching occupancy, retail and F&B filling in around the residential base, and the MRT interchange becoming a genuinely central transit node for the wider Klang Valley rather than just a TRX-specific stop.

That's a reasonable bet given the scale of institutional commitment already made to the district, but it does carry more execution-timeline uncertainty than investing in an already fully-established district like KL City Centre — which is exactly the trade-off between TRX's growth potential and KL City Centre's proven stability that we covered in our KL City Centre vs TRX comparison.
