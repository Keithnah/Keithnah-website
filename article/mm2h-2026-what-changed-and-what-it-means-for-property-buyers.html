MM2H 2026: What Changed and What It Means for Property Buyers

28 Jun 2026 · Keith Nah

Malaysia My Second Home (MM2H) has gone through several rounds of revision in recent years, and 2026's framework looks meaningfully different from the version many buyers researched a few years ago. For property buyers specifically, the changes affect not just who qualifies, but how MM2H status interacts with a property purchase.

What's Changed In The Current Framework

The current MM2H framework is tiered, with different financial requirements and stay conditions depending on the applicant's chosen tier. Compared to the original long-running programme, today's version generally asks for higher proof of liquid assets and, for some tiers, a minimum property purchase or a fixed deposit placed in Malaysia as part of the qualifying conditions.

This has narrowed the applicant pool somewhat compared to the programme's earlier, more accessible years, but it has also stabilised the programme's reputation after a period of uncertainty — applicants today generally have more clarity on requirements than during the transitional years when rules were being actively revised.

How MM2H Interacts With Property Ownership

MM2H status itself is a long-stay visa programme, not a separate property ownership category — an MM2H participant still purchases property under the same foreign ownership rules and state price thresholds that apply to any other foreign buyer. The main practical link is that some MM2H tiers accept a qualifying property purchase as part of meeting the programme's financial requirements, which can make sense for buyers who were planning to purchase a home in Malaysia regardless.

For buyers using property to help meet MM2H conditions, it's important to confirm the current minimum qualifying property value and any restrictions on property type before committing, since these thresholds have moved with each revision of the programme and can differ from the general foreign-buyer minimum purchase price for the state.

What This Means For Buyers Today

For buyers primarily interested in property as an investment rather than a long-stay visa, the MM2H changes are largely irrelevant — a standard foreign purchase doesn't require MM2H status at all. But for buyers weighing a longer-term relocation alongside their purchase, it's worth having an MM2H-current advisor confirm the latest tier requirements before finalising a unit, since qualifying property values and financial thresholds have shifted more than once in recent years.

We coordinate with MM2H-focused advisors for clients exploring this path, since property selection and visa eligibility are genuinely separate processes that happen to intersect — and getting the sequencing wrong (buying before confirming a property qualifies, for instance) can create avoidable complications.
