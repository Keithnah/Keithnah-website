Accent PJ: Is a Private Lift Lobby the New Condo Standard?

16 Sep 2026 · Keith Nah

A growing number of new Petaling Jaya launches are marketing "private lift lobby" access — where a lift opens directly into a small landing serving only one or two units per floor, rather than a shared corridor lined with multiple doors. Accent PJ is one of the more visible projects pushing this feature, and it's worth understanding what it actually changes for buyers, beyond the marketing appeal.

What Private Lift Lobby Actually Means

A standard condo floor might have six to ten units branching off one shared corridor. Private-lift-lobby designs cut that to two to four units per lift stop, sometimes fewer — effectively borrowing a layout convention from ultra-luxury towers and bringing it into the upper-mid to high-end segment.

The appeal isn't purely cosmetic. Visitors and deliveries no longer pass a string of other units' front doors on the way in, which is a genuine privacy and security upgrade that buyers who've lived in Singapore or Hong Kong developments often expect as a baseline.

The Trade-Offs Developers and Buyers Face

Fewer units sharing each lift core usually means higher land-and-construction cost per saleable square foot. Buyers are effectively paying a premium for that layout efficiency trade-off, not just for finishes or branding.

Maintenance costs tend to run higher too, since fewer units share the same lift and lobby infrastructure and common-area upkeep. That's worth factoring into service-charge expectations rather than comparing units purely on purchase price.

Is It a Lasting Trend or a Launch-Cycle Gimmick

Private lift lobby has moved from a handful of ultra-luxury KL City Centre towers down into more accessible PJ addresses over the past few launch cycles, which suggests genuine buyer demand rather than a one-off marketing push for a single project.

That said, not every project needs it. For smaller units or lower price points, the added cost may not translate into a resale premium, so the feature tends to matter most in the mid-to-large unit, higher-price-tier segment where buyers already expect a degree of exclusivity.
