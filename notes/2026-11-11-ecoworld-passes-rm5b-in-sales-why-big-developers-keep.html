EcoWorld passes RM5b in sales: why big developers keep selling

Eco World Development Group's sales for its financial year passed RM5 billion for the first time, after it agreed a land sale worth about RM1.01 billion to Tera Data Centers. Before that deal, the group's sales had already reached about RM4.05 billion by 31 August 2026, above its full-year target of RM4 billion. The company reported higher profit for its third quarter, and research houses have said industrial demand, including land for data centres, is a key driver of its growth. Management said the group is on track for a strong finish to its financial year, which ended on 31 October 2026, and the full-year results are still to come. The result fits a wider pattern this year: large, established developers with a long record of delivering projects have kept selling well, even as housing loans have become harder to get. Part of EcoWorld's figure comes from industrial land rather than homes, so it is not a pure measure of housing demand.

Keith's take

For buyers, the lesson is not to buy EcoWorld or any one brand, but to weigh the developer as seriously as the unit. A developer's track record matters most when you buy off-plan, because you pay in stages for something that will only be finished years later. Before you book, check how many projects the developer has completed, whether they were handed over on time, and what owners say about build quality and defects. A listed developer also publishes its sales and finances, which gives you more to go on. Then compare the project on location, price per square foot and maintenance fees, because a strong brand does not make an overpriced unit a good buy. For investors, established developers often hold their value better on resale, since buyers trust the name. In my own listings, SWNK Houze at BBCC is one EcoWorld project you can look up.
