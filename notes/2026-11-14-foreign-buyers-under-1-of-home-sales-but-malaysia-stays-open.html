Foreign buyers: under 1% of home sales, but Malaysia stays open

Malaysia remains one of the most accessible property markets in Asia for foreign buyers, EdgeProp reported this week, even after the stamp duty on transfers for foreigners doubled to a flat 8% on 1 January 2026. Foreigners can generally own both freehold and leasehold homes in their own name. Foreign buyers are still a small part of the market: in the first half of 2025, non-citizens made 913 home purchases worth about RM1.9 billion, 0.76% of all transactions but 3.8% of their value. Those figures were given in the Dewan Rakyat in November 2025, with buyers from China (329 deals) and Singapore (320) leading the list. Rehda's Ho Hon Sang puts the foreign share at about 1% of residential deals by volume, based on the NAPIC Property Market Report 2025. The gap between the share by number and the share by value shows that foreign purchases are concentrated in higher-priced homes. Access comes with conditions set by each state: foreign buyers need state consent and must meet a minimum price, commonly RM1 million in Kuala Lumpur, while Bumiputera lots and low-cost or affordable homes are closed to them. Thresholds differ by state and sometimes by district or property type, so the figure for a specific unit has to be checked. The rules also change over time, as the 8% rate itself shows, so buyers should confirm the current position before they commit.

Keith's take

For a foreign buyer, the headline is good news: Malaysia still lets you own a home outright, freehold included, under clear rules you can plan around. Being under 1% of buyers also means you are not competing with a crowd of foreign money, because prices here are set mainly by local demand. The catch is that the rules work unit by unit. Before you fall for a project, check three things: the state minimum for that exact unit, whether the unit is approved for foreign buyers, and the full entry cost including 8% stamp duty and legal fees. For investors, focus on locations where locals also want to rent and buy, such as near MRT and LRT stations, because locals will be most of your tenants and your future buyers. My checklist: use the state check on my Foreign Buyers page, shortlist only units above the minimum, ask for a full cost sheet and plan to hold for the long term. If you also want to live here, look at MM2H separately, because buying a home does not come with a visa.
