Malton's Ukay Spring brings gated landed homes to Ampang

Malton Bhd has launched the first phase of Ukay Spring, a 56-acre development in Ukay Perdana, Ampang. Phase 1 has 142 stratified landed homes in a gated-and-guarded community. The mix is three-storey semi-detached homes on 40ft by 90ft plots and 2.5-storey link villas on 22ft by 80ft plots. According to EdgeProp, the phase comes with a clubhouse, jogging paths and linear parks, and the developer is selling it as low-density hillside living. Ukay Perdana is an established, leafy neighbourhood on the north-east edge of Kuala Lumpur, known for bungalows and quiet streets. New landed launches this close to the city are rare, which is why projects like this tend to get attention. The bigger picture is that most new supply in the Klang Valley is high-rise, while demand for landed homes from families stays steady. Hillside sites need extra care, though: slope works, drainage and access roads all matter. Since Phase 1 is only the start of a 56-acre plan, later phases will shape traffic and the feel of the area over the next few years.

Keith's take

I like seeing genuine landed supply this close to KL City Centre, because good family homes with a bit of garden are getting harder to find. Ampang's Ukay area has a loyal following, and a well-run gated project can hold its value nicely over time. For home buyers, this suits families who want space and security but don't want to move far out to Semenyih or Rawang. For investors, landed homes usually rent more slowly than city condos, so treat this as a long-term hold rather than a quick rental play. Before you commit, check a few things. Ask for the land tenure and the expected strata title and completion timeline in writing. Look at the slope protection and drainage plans, and visit after heavy rain. Ask about monthly maintenance fees, since strata landed homes pay for guards and shared facilities. Drive the route to your office at peak hour.
