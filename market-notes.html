Malaysia2026-11-10

Bank Negara holds the OPR at 2.75%: what it means for your home loan

Bank Negara Malaysia kept the Overnight Policy Rate (OPR) at 2.75% at its final Monetary Policy Committee meeting of 2026 on 5 November. It is the eighth meeting in a row without a change. The central bank said growth stayed strong in the third quarter, helped by exports and steady spending at home, and it now expects the economy to grow by more than 5% this year. Governor Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour said there is no immediate need to raise rates while inflation stays contained, but the bank is watching whether wage increases start to push prices up. The ringgit firmed after the decision, and the next meeting is on 21 January 2027.

Keith's take

For buyers, this means home loan rates should stay where they are for the next few months. If you are planning to buy, now is a good time to get a loan pre-approval and compare packages from two or three banks, because a stable rate gives you a clear monthly figure to plan around. At the same time, do not plan your budget to the last ringgit. Some economists still see a 0.25% rise in 2027, so check that you can comfortably pay a slightly higher instalment. Use the calculator to test your numbers at today's rate and at 0.25% higher before you commit.

Kuala Lumpur2026-11-10

JLL Q3 briefing: Kuala Lumpur property is recovering, slowly

Property consultancy JLL presented its third-quarter 2026 findings on 10 November and described the market as recovering gradually. The clearest trend is a move to quality: companies are relocating to newer, better-built offices, and that demand is lifting occupancy in the best buildings in Greater Kuala Lumpur. On the residential side, the number and value of home sales improved modestly in the second quarter after a sharp dip at the start of the year, but they were still slightly lower than a year earlier. JLL has said it expects homes prices to stay broadly stable this year, with affordability the biggest thing holding buyers back.

Keith's take

A slow recovery is good news for careful buyers. Prices are not running away, so you have time to compare projects, ask developers for their best terms and negotiate, instead of rushing in. The flight to quality in offices matters for homes too: the people who work in those better buildings want to rent nearby. Projects close to MRT and LRT stations, with good build quality and sensible maintenance fees, are the ones most likely to stay in demand. If you are buying to rent out, put location and quality ahead of the lowest price.
